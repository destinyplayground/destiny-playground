Developer Note

The purpose of these dev updates is to share systems currently in development and to give insight into the early stages of design as the project evolves. What you're seeing right now are the foundations and skeletons of systems, nowhere near their final versions.

Things like names, values, stats, and organisation are often placeholders or early iterations. My current focus is building the core structure and functionality of systems first. Refinement, balancing, and polish come later in development.

Because of this, please keep in mind that everything shown in dev updates is subject to change. Feedback and suggestions are always welcome, but constructive feedback with reasoning is far more helpful than simple reactions.

Get Involved

The team is smaller than it needs to be right now, and we've just updated both our builder and modeller application forms to reflect what we're currently looking for. These are the two areas where the project needs the most help heading into the next phase of development.

  • Builders — helping recreate Destiny locations in Minecraft. Experience with large-scale Minecraft builds is the main thing we're looking for.

Vault System

This week's main systems content. The Vault is your persistent gear storage — the place where everything you earn but aren't currently using lives between sessions. Getting it right matters, because it touches almost everything else: inventory management, loot chasing, loadout planning, and long-term progression.

What the Vault Is

The Vault is a shared, persistent storage space accessible from the Tower and the Last City. It holds weapons, armour, and materials — anything you want to keep but don't have equipped or in your immediate inventory. It persists between sessions, between prestige resets, and between seasons. Your Vault is yours.

It is not the same as your character inventory. Your character carries a limited number of items at any given time. The Vault is where the rest lives.

Capacity

Vault space is finite and meaningful. You start with a base capacity and can expand it through progression.

Base Capacity
Available from the start. Enough space to store a working collection of gear without feeling immediately pressured to dismantle everything.
Expanded Capacity
Unlocked through progression. Vault space can be increased by hitting specific milestones — campaign completion, rank thresholds, and potentially prestige milestones as the prestige system develops. Specific values to be confirmed closer to launch.
Prestige Expansion
Under consideration. Certain prestige milestones may unlock additional Vault space as a reward. This is being evaluated alongside the prestige system and will be confirmed when that system is finalised.

Organisation

The Vault is organised by item type — weapons, armour, and materials each have their own section. Within each section, items are sorted by rarity and power level by default, with the ability to manually reorder and flag items you want to keep track of.

The goal is a Vault that never requires you to scroll through a wall of identical items looking for the one thing you want. Organisation should feel effortless — the system should do most of the work for you.

Access Points

The Vault is accessible from two locations at launch:

  • The Tower — via the Vault terminal, the same location D1 players will recognise.
  • The Last City — via a second access point in the social hub area.

Access from the field — pulling items from the Vault while on a destination — is not currently planned for launch. If that changes, it'll be covered in a future update.

Dismantling

Items in the Vault can be dismantled directly without transferring them to your character first. Dismantling returns materials — Glimmer for common and uncommon gear, Marks for rares and legendaries, Exotic Shards for exotics. The Vault is also where dismantling exotic duplicates makes the most sense — keep the roll you want, dismantle the rest for Shards.

"The Vault should never be a source of stress. It's your gear library — the place where everything you've earned is waiting for you when you need it."

What Doesn't Go in the Vault

Currencies, quest items, bounties, and consumables live in your character's dedicated inventory slots rather than the Vault. The Vault is strictly for gear. This keeps the space clean and prevents the Vault from becoming a second inventory to manage.

Season Pass items and Eververse purchases go directly to character inventory on acquisition — they don't land in the Vault by default, though you can move them there manually.

All values and specifics here are subject to change as the system is built and tested. The structure is locked — the exact numbers come with playtesting.
